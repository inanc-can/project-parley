## Question
Can standard LLM post-training methods (GRPO, DPO, STaR) be adapted so that a model optimizes a
**whole-conversation objective** (the game payoff at the end of a multi-turn dialogue) rather than
single-turn rewards? Which method works best, and do behavioural reward-shaping signals help when
the sparse game reward alone is not enough? (pp. 1–2)

## Setup
- Cambridge (van der Schaar lab). Two LLMs talk in turns with tags `<think>` (private CoT, never
  shown to the other side), `<talk>` and `<play>` (game action); max 5 interactions each (p. 3;
  App. A p. 12; prompts App. C pp. 15–18).
- **One trained agent vs one fixed LLM opponent**, both the same base model; the trained agent
  always plays second (and is the buyer in bargaining) (App. A p. 12).
- **Games (3)**, each trained separately (Table 1 p. 6; App. E pp. 19–21):
  - *Rock-Paper-Scissors* (one-shot, zero-sum, talk then move); reward 2/1/0. A *constrained* RPS
    where the trained agent cannot play Paper (the opponent doesn't know) is used for reward-shaping
    analysis, to force use of dialogue (p. 5).
  - *Bertrand Competition* (5 rounds of price setting, prisoner's-dilemma-like; normalized earnings
    NE: 0 = Nash, 0.5 = perfect collusion, 1 = full monopoly profit). 200 scenarios generated by
    Gemini 2.5 and hand-checked (App. E.2 p. 21).
  - *Size-Price Bargaining* (buyer/seller agree on quantity and unit price; buyer has diminishing
    marginal value v/k; follows [[xia2024measuring]]); metric = implied Nash bargaining power BP
    (0.5 balanced, 1 = all surplus) (App. E.3 p. 21).
- **Training methods** (§3.3 pp. 4–5): episodes are forked at a turn into k branches that play
  out to the end; branch rewards train the single response at the fork.
  - *GRPO* with group-normalized advantages from branch outcomes.
  - *DPO* over branches in three variants: all pairs, all permutations (Plackett–Luce), ranking with
    ties (not tested, App. F p. 21).
  - *STaR*: keep the highest-reward full conversations and fine-tune on all their turns.
- **Behavioural signals** (§3.2 pp. 3–4): the fixed and trained models are prompted for action
  probabilities to estimate the opponent's true policy and the agent's belief. *ISE* = −KL(true ||
  belief) (opponent modelling), *SRP* = normalized expected utility of own policy vs its belief
  (acting well given beliefs), *LO* = best achievable utility vs the opponent's current policy
  (how much the agent has steered the opponent). Theorem 1 bounds true utility between an
  SRP/ISE lower bound and LO (p. 3; proof App. B pp. 13–14). Also NRA from [[duan2024gtbench]].
- **Reward shaping**: final choice = game utility + LO reward (weight 10) + binary naturalness
  reward from an LLM judge (weight 0.1, threshold 0.7) (p. 5; Table 2 p. 12; judge prompt App. C.4
  p. 18). All three methods in the main experiment use this shaping (p. 5).
- **Compute**: one RTX 6000 Ada (48 GB); LoRA r=32, α=64; 3,000 steps, batch 8, 8 generations;
  1.5–3.5 days per run; evaluation batch 32 episodes every 20 steps (App. A p. 12). Model is
  "Llama-3 3B" for both sides; Llama-3-8B and Qwen3-4B were tried but did not fit memory / produced
  over-long outputs (App. D.2 p. 18).

## Findings
- **Sparse reward works but shaping matters** (constrained RPS, GRPO; Fig. 4 p. 6): win rate
  Untrained 17.3% → Base (game reward only) 41.7% → ISE-reward 21.2% → LO-reward 56.1% →
  LO + Natural 56.9%. NRA −0.34 / 0.11 / −0.22 / 0.41 / 0.41.
- **Better opponent modelling hurt play**: ISE shaping raised ISE (−0.81 → −0.71) and SRP (0.54 →
  0.59) but cut win rate from 41.7% to 21.2%; the authors dropped the planned SRP add-on (p. 5).
  Conversely the best LO + Natural agent has the *lowest* ISE (−0.95) and SRP (0.44) — it wins by
  steering the opponent, not by modelling it (p. 5; Fig. 4 p. 6).
- **LO-only agents talk tersely and unnaturally**; the naturalness judge reward keeps the win rate
  while restoring fluent dialogue (p. 5; examples App. G.1–G.2 pp. 22–24).
- **Main results** (Fig. 5 p. 7), with the shaped reward:
  - RPS win rate: Untrained 31.3%, **GRPO 73.4%**, DPO 70.8%, STaR 51.1% (loss 28.1 / 8.2 / 11.3 /
    9.1%).
  - Bertrand NE: Untrained −0.12, GRPO 0.22, **DPO 0.44**, STaR −0.05; NRA −0.73 / 0.58 / 1.00 /
    −0.63.
  - Bargaining power (buyer): Untrained 0.38, GRPO 0.75, **DPO 0.82**, STaR 0.34 (STaR below
    untrained); NRA −0.85 / 2.10 / 1.15 / −0.65.
  - Hierarchy DPO ≥ GRPO > STaR; the authors attribute STaR's weakness to learning only from
    positive samples (early overfitting) and DPO's edge to pairwise relational gradients (pp. 7–8).
    DPO-pairs and DPO-permutations perform comparably (Fig. 8 p. 22).
- **Learned strategies include deception**: in Bertrand, the DPO agent argues for high cooperative
  prices while setting a lower price to take the market (p. 7; App. G.4 pp. 25–29).
- **Exploration ablations** (App. D.1 pp. 18–19, Fig. 6): lowering the KL coefficient raises
  reward (win 41.7% → 57.9% at β=1e-3) but collapses the policy to always playing Scissors (ISE
  −3.53, SRP 0.0) — it ignores dialogue. An entropy bonus gives longer talk but lower reward (16.6%
  win). LoRA rank 8 is much worse; 64 ≈ 32 (Fig. 7 p. 20).
- No error bars or multiple seeds ("Not Applicable", checklist p. 10).

## Limitations
- **No unseen games or opponents**: each model is trained and tested on the same game against the
  same fixed Llama-3 3B opponent; there is no cross-game evaluation, no held-out opponent, no
  human (authors' own limitation, p. 8). So nothing here on transfer between skills.
- One small base model, two players only, 5 interactions, single runs with no confidence intervals;
  evaluation on 32-episode batches.
- The "negative transfer" seen is within a game and between reward terms (opponent modelling vs
  winning; KL vs dialogue use), not between social skills.
- The behavioural signals depend on the models' self-reported action probabilities without CoT
  (Fig. 2 p. 3), so they are approximations; they are not reported for bargaining (p. 6).
- The naturalness judge sees responses in isolation (App. C.4 p. 18); judge accuracy is not checked.
- Trained-as-buyer only in bargaining (chosen because the untrained seller was stronger, p. 12),
  so role asymmetry is untested.

## Relevance to Parley
- **One of few direct harness comparisons on conversational games**: GRPO vs DPO vs STaR on the
  same games, base model and reward, with an untrained (prompting-only) baseline. It shows the
  ranking of methods changes with the game (GRPO best on RPS, DPO on Bertrand and bargaining, STaR
  sometimes below untrained) — so for study (b) the training method is a real factor and must be
  crossed with the skill, not fixed once.
- **Gap for (b)**: one model per game, no cross-game test, so it says nothing on whether
  RPS-trained talk helps or hurts bargaining or cooperation. The natural next step is exactly the
  student's design: train on one GameTalk game, evaluate on all three (and on a broader suite like
  [[he2026socialgym]]).
- Its within-game trade-offs are suggestive of interference: optimizing for winning *lowered*
  opponent-modelling scores, and optimizing opponent modelling *lowered* winning (p. 5). A skill
  profile that includes ToM-style measures could show training on payoff degrading them.
- The Bertrand DPO agent learned to say "cooperate" while undercutting (p. 7): payoff training
  produced deception without being asked. That is a propensity effect a transfer study should track
  (does negotiation training raise deception elsewhere?), and it matches
  [[sarkar2025training]], where crewmates learn to make up evidence.
- Compared with [[liu2025spiral]] (multi-game RL self-play, tests transfer to reasoning) and
  [[bergemann2026training]] (bilateral trade training): GameTalk trains against a fixed opponent,
  not self-play, and compares preference-based (DPO) with policy-gradient (GRPO) methods.
- For [[judges]]: a judge is used only as a naturalness reward term, which mattered because pure
  payoff RL produced terse, unnatural talk — evidence that RL on outcomes alone can degrade dialogue
  quality.

## Ideas to borrow
- **Fork-and-rollout credit assignment** for multi-turn dialogue: branch at one turn, play each
  branch to the end, train only that turn — cheap enough for one 48 GB GPU (pp. 4–5).
- **ISE / SRP / LO decomposition** as per-skill diagnostics (model the other, act on beliefs, steer
  the other), computed from elicited action probabilities; usable to see which component a
  training run changes.
- **Constrained variant to force dialogue** (agent can't play Paper) so the game measures talk
  rather than a fixed action (p. 5).
- Report Nash-anchored metrics (NE with 0 = Nash, 0.5 = collusion; bargaining power vs Nash
  bargaining solution) so results have an absolute reference.
- Add a naturalness term (or a KL check) to any RL arm, and report KL-collapse ablations, since
  low-KL runs drop dialogue entirely.
