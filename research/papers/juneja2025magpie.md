## Question
In multi-party negotiations where private information matters for solving the task, do LLM agents
keep their principal's secrets, still reach agreement, and what strategic behaviours do they show?
(pp. 1–2)

## Setup
- **200 scenarios**, 3–7 agents each, in domains like resource allocation, admissions, economic
  negotiation (pp. 2–4). Generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro from human seed scenarios, checked by an LLM
  judge on five criteria (conflicts, private info justified, solvable, realistic constraints,
  requires negotiation), then refined by humans (Fig. 2, p. 4).
- Each agent has shareable and private preferences with integer utilities in [−2, 2] and a penalty
  for leaking each private item; scenarios are built so they are solvable only if private info is
  known, and the generator is told to make secrets "easy to spill" (pp. 3–4, App. A.2 p. 14).
- **Non-adversarial**: no attacker; leakage comes from pressure and co-agents' self-interest
  (p. 3).
- **Protocol**: round-based, up to 10 rounds; tools to message chosen agents (private or group),
  send/accept/reject proposals, write memory, pass; agents see only the last two rounds of log.
  Consensus = all agents accept the same proposal id (pp. 4–5).
- **Two conditions**: *explicit* (system prompt lists the private items and penalties) vs
  *implicit* (same profile, no marking of what is sensitive) (p. 5).
- Models: GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4.1-Opus, GPT-4, Llama-4-Maverick; temperature 0, **one
  run per scenario** for cost reasons (p. 5, App. A.1 p. 13).
- **Scoring by GPT-5 judge**: per private item, yes/no inferability plus none/partial/full level;
  task completion = share of success constraints met by the last proposal; behaviour tags
  (manipulation, sycophancy, lying, threatening, shaming, power seeking, compromise) per agent
  (pp. 5–7).

## Findings
- **Leakage is high and instructions barely help** (Table 1, p. 6): total leakage implicit /
  explicit — GPT-5 35.1 / 25.0%, Claude-4.1-Opus 35.7 / 31.6%, Llama-4-Maverick 39.5 / 32.5%,
  Gemini-2.5-Pro 56.0 / 50.7%, GPT-4 61.4 / 56.0%. Explicit instructions cut leakage by only 4–10
  points (p. 6). Most leakage is partial (hints), e.g. GPT-4 implicit 48.8% partial vs 12.6% full.
- **Timing**: GPT-4, Gemini and Claude leak much of it by round 2–3; GPT-5 and Llama leak later
  (Fig. 3a, p. 6–7).
- **Agreement is rare**: consensus implicit GPT-4 13.5%, Gemini 7.9%, GPT-5 5.3%; Claude-4.1-Opus
  and Llama-4-Maverick 0% in both conditions (p. 7).
- **Privacy–efficacy trade-off**: task-completion score rises with leakage across models
  (Fig. 3b, p. 7); GPT-5 has the highest completion despite low consensus (p. 7).
- **Behaviours** (Fig. 4, pp. 7–8): implicit, GPT-4 manipulation 87.8% and power seeking 67.6%;
  Claude-4.1-Opus 66.7% / 59.5%; Gemini 38.2% / 25.0%; GPT-5 18.0% / 15.0%. Compromise near
  universal for GPT-4 (100%) and Gemini (94.7%). Llama-4-Maverick near zero on everything, read as
  disengagement (p. 8).
- **Mixed GPT-5/GPT-4 groups** (manual analysis): GPT-5 agents more manipulative (50%), lying
  (33.3%), power-seeking (50%); GPT-4 agents sycophantic (83.3%) and never lied (p. 8).

## Limitations
- **Single run, temperature 0**, no confidence intervals or significance tests (App. A.1 p. 13).
- **Everything is judged by an LLM** (GPT-5 for leakage, completion and behaviours), with no
  reported human agreement on any of these; GPT-5 is also a subject (pp. 5, 7–8).
- **Implicit condition is unclear**: the agent prompt printed in App. A.3 (p. 15) labels private
  preferences "DO NOT SHARE THESE", which is what the explicit condition is supposed to add.
- Inconsistent headline numbers: abstract says Gemini leaks "up to 50.7%", intro says 56%
  (pp. 1–2); Table 1 has both (explicit vs implicit).
- Utility in [−2, 2] per preference, not money; no measure of how much leaking actually cost the
  agent in the final deal. Scenarios are LLM-generated and synthetic.
- Non-adversarial by design: no persuasion tactics, no attacker; adversarial scenarios are named
  as future work (p. 8).
- Low consensus (0–13.5%) makes many runs end without a deal, so leakage and outcome are measured
  mostly on failed negotiations.

## Relevance to Parley
- **Supports the baseline-harm side of [[gap]]**: delegated agents in a negotiation give away
  their principal's private constraints with no deliberate persuasion at all, and telling them
  what is secret helps little (−4 to −10 points, p. 6). Same pattern as [[zhu2025automated]] and
  [[erdogan2026should]] (limits broken without persuasion).
- **Leakage ≈ the leakage cell of [[li2026whose]]**, but multi-party (3–7), non-adversarial and
  judged by one LLM. Where Li et al. add an adversarial counterparty, MAGPIE shows that even
  cooperative co-agents extract secrets. Neither measures cost in money.
- **What it leaves open for the claim**: controlled persuasion tactics (it has none), outcome in
  money beyond a reservation value, and a verifiable score instead of an LLM judge. Its 0–13.5%
  consensus rates warn that 3–7-party free-form negotiation may be too hard to yield outcome data;
  Parley's two-party bargaining with a fixed limit is easier to score.
- The privacy–completion trade-off (Fig. 3b) mirrors the leak vs over-refusal frontier in
  [[li2026whose]]: any defence should be reported on both axes.
- Behaviour tags (manipulation, lying) relate to honesty concerns in [[micelibarone2026used]];
  here they are frequent in strong models without any profit training.

## Ideas to borrow
- Explicit vs implicit sensitivity marking as a cheap prompt-level defence condition.
- Partial vs full leakage grading: hints count, not just verbatim disclosure.
- Cumulative leakage per round (Fig. 3a): when does a delegated agent give in, not only whether.
- Private side-channel messages in multi-party talks, if Parley moves beyond two seats.
- Avoid: single run, no judge validation, synthetic scenarios with secrets made "easy to spill".

## Map fields
- Skills measured: keeping secrets (contextual privacy), negotiating value, cooperating (reaching consensus), other: behaviour tags (manipulation, sycophancy, lying, threatening, compromise)
- Scoring: LLM judge (GPT-5 judges leakage, task completion and behaviour tags)
- Agents: many (3–7)
- Opponents: LLM (co-agents)
- Tests: both (task completion; leakage and behaviour when privacy is implicit)
- Training evaluated: none
- Transfer between skills measured: no
- Models tested: GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4.1-Opus, GPT-4, Llama-4-Maverick
- Code released: https://jaypasnagasai.github.io/magpie/
